Work

Portfolio

  1. NVIDIA2026

    GPU power model

    40 engineers use it every day. A run used to take 1 min 55 s, and now it takes 60 µs.

    My team works out how much power a GPU will draw long before there’s any silicon to measure. That used to mean a spreadsheet with one row per configuration and a queue of batch jobs, and later a desktop tool.

    A run is one what-if: change a clock or a voltage and get the power back for every block and rail. I rewrote the tool from scratch in TypeScript on Bun and SvelteKit, in about 60 days (1,044 commits). Each what-if becomes a small program that only redoes the steps whose inputs changed. The web app and the Python client share that one engine, so a number in a notebook and a number on screen always agree.

    The speed came from the algorithm, not from threads. I built a worker pool, measured it and deleted it. I also gave up bit-for-bit identical answers for speed, so a slower reference engine checks every compiled answer.

  2. NVIDIA2025

    PACE

    A chatbot over our team’s docs. Twelve interns ramped up on it and rated it 9.5/10.

    Our team’s know-how was spread across a codebase and a lot of Confluence pages, and new interns had to dig through all of it. PACE stands for Power Architecture Chat Engine. It answers their questions from those pages, using OpenWebUI, ChromaDB, LiteLLM and FastAPI.

    When it gave a wrong answer, it was usually because the page behind it was outdated, so we fixed the pages too. It won NTech 2025, NVIDIA’s internal paper competition. I built it so agent workflows could sit on top of it next.

  3. 2026

    Lokasu

    My own studio. lokasu.com and its free Launch Check are live.

    I designed and coded lokasu.com in SvelteKit and Three.js. Thirteen 3D models share the same 512 parts and morph from one to the next, and your cursor pushes them around. Launch Check runs on a Cloudflare Worker. Paste a link and in under 20 seconds it tells you what will break when you share it, like a missing preview image or a dead link. It doesn’t need an account.

  4. 2026

    Vilje Tech

    AI and automation for Nordic companies. I build ViljePDF, ViljeCAD and Inwoice.

    ViljePDF reads engineering drawings that have no text layer, shows every value next to the spot it came from so a person can check it, and compares two revisions line by line. It runs locally on ONNX Runtime with no cloud calls. On held-out public datasets it scores F1 0.99 on GD&T frames and parts lists.

    ViljeCAD turns drawing PDFs into a 3D model, a floor plan, a bill of materials and an IFC file. The code measures every line on the sheet and the AI model decides what each line means. Its accuracy is checked against the measurements printed on the drawing, to 0.01 mm.

    Inwoice takes Swedish supplier invoices from the inbox to Fortnox or Visma. AI reads each invoice, and bookkeeping rules plus a person’s approval decide what gets posted. It’s built against a spec of 113 features, and every one of them is tied to a test.

  5. College2024

    StarCraft II paper

    My final-year project on StarCraft II tactics, published by Springer.

    Instead of learning the game one click at a time, our agent chose between bigger tactics, using a knowledge-infused policy gradient with UCB. Across five mini-games it beat DeepMind’s baseline agent at gathering resources and training units, and lost badly in fights. Written at NIT Andhra Pradesh and published in the ICTIS 2025 proceedings in January 2026.
